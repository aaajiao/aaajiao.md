## Relations

This sequence joins digital identity to the material track of the practice. It touches factory skill and **Absorption / 入神**, club culture and **Trance / 出神**, Internet Void, platform governance, global logistics, and the artist’s Berlin–Shanghai position.

## Current response

**Player / 玩家** is not a consumer of a game, nor an already free subject. A Player remains inside rules while learning their rhythm, limits, and possible points of movement through action, trial, and judgment.

**Void-Seeker / 虚空探索者** is a method rather than a stable identity. It looks for behaviours, affects, and relations that a system cannot fully name, quantify, or absorb: a craftsperson’s embodied judgment, a temporary nocturnal collectivity, or an inequality of fate that does not appear in a data model. The task is not to own the Void, but to keep it perceptible.

**Void / 虚空** is neither blankness, failure, nor utopia. In *The Eve of Parasitism*, it is a temporary shelter that appears under pressure. The bullet-hell mechanic of **grazing**—Chinese: **擦弹**; Japanese: **グレイズ**—gives it a precise form: a projectile passes close enough to register danger without hitting the player. This moment does not overturn the system or promise permanent exit. It makes agency perceptible again.

## Tensions and counterexamples

- Does the language of shelter risk making precarity appear survivable or desirable?
- Does a player’s learned agency translate to material conditions where the cost of failure is distributed unequally?
- Can the work keep the Void hidden enough to remain an encounter rather than an illustrated concept?

## Next interview questions

- What changes when grazing is a felt audiovisual event rather than an explained game mechanic?
- When does the Player become an observer again, and what does that loss of agency reveal?
- Which elements of factory or club life should resist becoming evidence for the concept card?
