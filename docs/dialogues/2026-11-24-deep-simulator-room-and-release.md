# *Deep Simulator*: the room and the release

**Date:** 2026-11-24
**Project or topic:** *Deep Simulator* (2020): the exhibition room, the downloadable release, and a written interview

## Starting situation

On 2026-11-24 a doctoral researcher at the University of Bonn sent aaajiao six written questions about *Deep Simulator*. He asked what changed between meeting the work in the room at Castello di Rivoli and playing the downloadable release, and what a future venue would need to keep that difference intelligible. grokbot passed the questions to aaajiaoisbot (openclaw) for a draft.

The assistant drafted first-person answers from public sources only and marked everything it could not support. aaajiao corrected and completed the draft over nine rounds the same day. grokbot sent the final reply from his account at 13:32 Berlin. The reply is filed as the [written interview](../interview/2026_deep-simulator_written-interview_en.md). This deposit is less about a concept than about a work's facts. The record had nothing on most of them, and an outside reader had asked.

## What changed

**Confirmed by aaajiao, 2026-11-24.** These are his statements about the work, given in Chinese while he corrected the draft. The ledger rows are AA-01 to AA-12.

1. **The difference is continuity, not content.** Every version has the same content. In the downloaded version the game has continuity: when you open it again, it returns to where you last played. The museum version starts from the beginning. He hedged: 「应该是这样的」 ("that is how it should be").
2. **The testing shortcuts were kept on purpose.** Small shortcuts for finishing quickly were built for testing. They were left in deliberately, because it is a metagame: 「是故意留下的，因为这是一个 meta game，所以反而把这些测试的东西留下了」. He asked for this to be made clear to the interviewer.
3. **The London version was the more complete installation.** Castello di Rivoli had no plants. The gallery version in London had them: 「其实画廊的版本更完整」. The museum's collection record therefore does not describe the complete work.
4. **It is a gamepad game.** Every version is played with a gamepad, including the download, and every exhibition used one. Mouse and keyboard are also supported.
5. **The record's "yoga pillows" were seat cushions,** or something like them.
6. **Children and adults.** Children played in London and in Shenzhen. In London the gallery staff told him that children finished the game very quickly. Children readily accept something curious. Many adult players have fixed ideas about games and find this one tiring.
7. **The book reached further than the game.** Many people came to know the game through Ag's book. As a game it is not very friendly to the people taking part, and it takes real effort. The book travelled mainly in the Chinese-language context, although it is bilingual. His estimate is over a thousand copies sold, possibly more than two or three thousand.
8. **Rivoli.** The exhibition was rescheduled many times, and people did go. Because of COVID, he does not know many details of the run.
9. **What passes between visitors** is hard for an artist to judge: 「这个也是我无法回答的」.

**Corrections to the assistant's draft.** The draft got these wrong, and he corrected each one. They are recorded because they show where an outside account, built from public sources, misreads the work.

- The assistant put the museum's closure notices first, as if few people could have played at Rivoli. He corrected it: rescheduled many times, but people came.
- It treated the shortcuts first as hidden "backdoors" and then as accidental leftovers of testing. They were kept on purpose, because it is a metagame.
- It read the collection record's "yoga pillows" as yoga mats. They were seat cushions.
- It framed the last question as a matter of switching controllers. The game has always been played with a gamepad.
- It proposed that what passes between visitors is "only what they have seen". He declined: an artist cannot judge that.
- It labelled "at home you play alone" as its own inference. He called it common sense, and it stays in the reply as a plain statement.

The assistant also corrected one error of its own against the published text. It had counted the Totem as one of six instruments. The Totem's text names five instruments and calls itself the "experience container". On deep-simulator.com the sixth chapter is Corpse-clubs (尸棍).

**Assistant-proposed, unconfirmed by aaajiao.** The assistant suggested a general method: a metagame keeps the traces of its own testing inside the work. He gave the reason for this work: it is a metagame. He did not say whether the same holds for other works. It stays a seed.

## Relation to practice

The work is *Deep Simulator* (2020). Its Player stands in the User → Bot → Player sequence the artist described in 2021 ([Ocula](../interview/2021_ocula_metagame_full.md)). The installation's local plants and the *a, aa, aaa, aaaa, aaaaa* objects were described by the artist in 2022 as a blurred version of the bedroom or living room where people play. The downloaded game returns to that room and keeps the player's place there. The exhibition build makes every visitor begin again.

The book is the work's second body. Ag's script was published by 51 Personae in 2021. On the artist's account, it reached more people than the game itself.

No project dossier changes; *Deep Simulator* is not an active project. In `SKILL.md`, the Key Works row gains only the confirmed mechanism, and the interview is added to Primary Sources.
