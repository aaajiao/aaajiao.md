**What the review supports.** This is not a position of aaajiao's. It is Merve Emre, "The Lasting Influence of John Berger," *The New Yorker*, published online 9 November 2026 (issue of 16 November 2026), a review of Tom Overton, *John Berger: From Life* (Verso). The page was read on 2026-11-14. Overton's book, the films, and the novels were not. Berger was born on 5 November 1926; the review marks his centennial. Limits and the split between quotation and paraphrase are in the [source ledger](../research/2026-11-14-john-berger-source-ledger.md).

- Emre's account of Berger, not a quotation from him: the artist did not simply perceive the world; the world trained the artist's perceptions, teaching him what to look at, from what perspectives, and in what proportions. The artist could wield his brush against the world's accepted values, its tendency to see people from a unitary, fixed perspective.
- *Ways of Seeing* (BBC, 1972). Emre calls episode 1 an elegant distillation of Walter Benjamin's "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction." She writes that Berger explained that images of art had become "ephemeral, ubiquitous, insubstantial, available, valueless, free."
- Episode 2, as Emre quotes it: "A woman must continually watch herself." Emre then gives his distinction in her own sentence for the first half and his words for the second: to be naked is simply to be oneself, but to be nude is "to be seen naked by others and yet not recognized for oneself."
- Emre on the novels: they draw on Cubism's overlapping perspectives. She names *A Painter of Our Time* (1958) and *G.* (1972) among a longer list. Her sentence on their common subject — human perception, what Berger heralded as "the functioning of sight itself" — covers that list, not only the two titles named here.
- Janos, in *A Painter of Our Time*, is Emre's "kind of double émigré": he cannot bear the corruption and greed of London's curators and collectors, and he has no desire to return to Budapest and become a "professional revolutionary," surrendering his canvases to socialist-realist kitsch. "Double émigré" is Emre's phrase.
- The review, recounting Overton, says that when *G.* won the Booker Prize, Berger found the award intolerable because it had been endowed by a family whose wealth came from plantations in Guyana, and that he pledged half his winnings to the Black Panthers of Britain. A speech text was not checked.
- Emre's critique, hers and not Berger's. His Marxist aesthetic is "less a doctrine than a vague and ennobling justification" for why he liked the art that he did. After the move to Quincy in 1974, the peasant trilogy *Into Their Labors* is, in her judgment, no longer a way of seeing, sharply and dialectically, but "a way of living, nostalgically." Berger, she writes, called the pastoral turn in interviews an outgrowth of his "Romantic Marxism"; she says that if he typified anything it was not that, but what G. M. Tamás called a "Rousseauian socialist." On his treatment of wives and children, she says the conduct itself was hardly a shock; what shocked her is that Overton absolves him. She quotes Overton: "Perhaps you didn't do what was expected of contemporary fathers, but you were there." She ends by wishing for "a little more pessimism of the intellect," set against "a desperate optimism of the will." The review does not name Antonio Gramsci. The pair is his formula.

**Assistant-proposed, unconfirmed by aaajiao.** The assistant's reading of what in Berger might matter here. He has not adopted, revised, or rejected any of it. Each item is a candidate, not a genealogy and not a claim that the practice comes from Berger.

1. **Assistant-proposed.** The likely core of the influence is the early Berger: the world trains perception, and the question of who decides how we see once images are ubiquitous and free. The assistant's compression of Emre's sentence is "the world trains your eyes." The further step is the assistant's alone: today that training is done by recommendation algorithms and platform interfaces, rather than by museums and wealth. That would be a next layer after *Ways of Seeing*, and a layer this practice works on. The existing terms that could be tested against it are Internet Void (overlooked behavior, once attention stays on content), algorithmic governance, and platform politics. The proposal does not say that those terms are Berger's, or that he is their source.
2. **Assistant-proposed.** The naked/nude distinction can be transposed to platforms: being seen without being recognized as oneself. Berger's sentence about the nude is not a sentence about platforms. The transposition is the assistant's.
3. **Assistant-proposed.** Janos's double-émigré position resembles aaajiao's position between Berlin and Shanghai. Emre's label for a character in a 1958 novel is not a description of this practice. A related comparison, *doikayt* / hereness with Berlin and Shanghai, was left open in the [2026-09-24 deposit](2026-09-end-times-fascism-and-shelter.md) and was not confirmed. This item does not answer that question.
4. **Assistant-proposed.** Emre's critique as a mirror. On her account, Berger moved from how to see to how to live. The assistant suggests that aaajiao's void, and Absorption / Trance, stay where seeing fails. That is a reading of the skill's terms, not a restatement of them. Internet Void is where overlooked behavior becomes visible. Absorption (入神) is focused craft. Trance (出神) is generational drift. "Where seeing fails" is the assistant's phrase.
