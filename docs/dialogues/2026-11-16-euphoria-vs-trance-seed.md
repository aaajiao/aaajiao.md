The next sentences: "Its excess is an infinite transgression of finite limits (laws, morality, resources, etc.) for some(one). It can only ever rejoice in temporary transgression and will immediately be disappointed in meeting the next limit. Tyrants are sad people." The briefing's "the tyrant is a sad person" is a paraphrase of that plural sentence. deLire then writes: "The excess of political surrealism, by contrast, experiences its own creative force as powerful." "Joy is the experience of an increase of power" is deLire's sentence. Footnote 59 says this is agreement with Spinoza, *Ethics* III, proposition 11, scholium (E3p11s). The *Ethics* was not read, so the sentence is not quoted here as Spinoza's. The briefing's "increase in power" is a paraphrase of "increase of power." deLire's name for the institution is "the surrealist state," which "funds fantasy gyms and brainstorming casinos." The briefing's "surreal state" and "brainstorm casinos" are paraphrases of those two phrases. "The utopianism of the surrealist state consists in ongoing collective utopian thinking" and "It does not envision a particular plan or program." The briefing's "renewed primitive accumulation" is a paraphrase of that sentence. deLire attributes "reverse centaurs" to Cory Doctorow, and glosses them, in deLire's sentence, as "organic servants to AI commanders." Doctorow's book was not read.

1. **Assistant-proposed.** The seed sets the euphoria of collective invention beside Trance (出神). In Core, Trance is a generation shaped by club culture, now drifting through economic contraction: bodies moving, souls elsewhere, not chosen escape but enforced disorientation. The seed's "trance imposed under economic contraction" uses that term's conditions. It does not restate the term. Core also says Absorption (入神) and Trance are opposites, and that they must not be confused. The seed does not identify euphoria with Absorption. Focused craft is not collective invention. Whether euphoria is a third state, or a misreading of one of the two, is open.
2. **Assistant-proposed.** "May look the same on a screen" is the seed's test, set against Internet Void. In Core, Internet Void is overlooked behavior, not content: what the screen holds is what gets looked at, and the behavior that produces it stays unseen. The assistant's further step is that two states can share a screen image and still differ in whether an exit remains, or whether future time remains. That step is the assistant's. It does not say the seed is the source of Internet Void, or that Internet Void already makes this distinction.
3. **Assistant-proposed.** The seed's "exit channel" sits near an open question that is already deposited and is not answered here. The [2026-09-24 deposit](2026-09-end-times-fascism-and-shelter.md) asks whether grazing is a practice of staying or the aesthetic of those who have no right to exit.
