## Current response

Use **decolonization** for the material dismantling of colonial rule, sovereignty, and institutions. Use **coloniality** for the continuing organisation of power, knowledge, perception, and value after formal colonial rule. Use **decolonial practice** for ongoing efforts to interrupt these arrangements; do not use it as a completed virtue claim.

In *The Eve of Parasitism*, **unexchangeable critique / 不可兑换的批判** names a specific asymmetry. Manufacturing skill can be converted into productive capacity and then into national-industrial force, without returning agency to workers. Cultural-political critique can acquire international circulation and symbolic value, without converting into local material-political efficacy. The phrase does not call cultural work useless. It describes its displaced efficacy.

The project therefore refuses two simplifications: that the Global South is a coherent innocent subject; and that access to global cultural language automatically produces local political agency. Decolonial practice begins by keeping those two false conversions visible.

## Tensions and counterexamples

- Does “unexchangeable critique” overstate the separation between cultural work and material politics, thereby repeating the very impotence it diagnoses?
- Does the language of coloniality flatten distinct histories of Hong Kong, China, Europe, and the Global South?
- Can a critique of Eurocentric institutional recognition avoid converting non-Western difference into another marketable sign?
- What would redistribution of authorial, classificatory, or perceptual power actually look like in the installation?
