# The Eve of Parasitism / 寄生前夜

**Status:** Testing  
**Former working title:** *Symbiosis / 共生*  
**Format:** Four-screen video installation  
**Origin:** Shanghai acrylic factory; Shanghai club ALL; global labour and supply-chain research

## Project

*The Eve of Parasitism* examines relationships that appear symbiotic but are structured by unequal dependence. The title retains a resonance with *Parasite Eve* while remaining distinct: the “eve” is not a countdown to one event but a long, inescapable condition in which change has not visibly arrived yet has already shaped everyday life.

The former title, *Symbiosis*, was too peaceful. It could not name the conflict inside intimacy, trust, production, and cultural desire. Parasitism here is not the opposite of symbiosis; it is the unequal relation concealed by its language.

## Three relations

1. **Factory and industrial chain.** Craft skill and ordinary goodwill are drawn into a manufacturing system that can be mobilised as geopolitical force.
2. **Studio and craftsmen.** The artist's studio is physically inside a Shanghai acrylic factory. The studio and factory look almost inseparable, and shared making produces real trust. Yet their social recognition, class position, access to knowledge and wealth, and future possibilities remain sharply different.
3. **Youth and city.** Young people invest energy, bodies, desire, and cultural capital in Shanghai's club life. The city consumes and depends on this vitality while global cultural promises do not change their historical predicament.

The work does not resolve these contradictions or claim to speak for the craftsmen. It constructs a scene in which their creativity can be perceived and the contradictions can remain active.

## Four screens

| Screen | Material | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Footage of craftsmen at work | Makes bodily technique, attention, and under-recognised creativity visible |
| 2 | The final two days of Shanghai club ALL, including a bullet-hell game sequence | Holds youth culture, desire, cultural consumption, and temporary escape together |
| 3 | First-person game | Lets a visitor enter without assigning a fixed identity; perception is carried by each player's experience |
| 4 | Data visualisation, histories of industrial transfer, global labour flows, a China–EU low-value parcel study, and 3D scans of the studio-factory | Makes logistics, tax thresholds, statistics, infrastructure, and the conversion of lived scenes into information visible |

The screens are not four explanations of the same thesis. They form an unstable field in which labour, urban affect, game agency, logistics, and information systems pressure one another.

The game and data layers have their own project repositories: [Danmaku](https://github.com/aaajiao/Danmaku) is the original browser bullet-hell work; [1bit](https://github.com/aaajiao/1bit) is the first-person 3D work; and [CHINA-EU-FLOW-ASCII](https://github.com/aaajiao/CHINA-EU-FLOW-ASCII) is the 1-bit ASCII visualisation of China–EU e-commerce parcel flows. They are not supplementary illustrations but working media through which the project’s digital and material tracks meet.

### Artificial flowers and the logistics field

All floral elements are purchased artificial flowers. They enter the installation as readymades rather than simulations of nature: cheap, bright, repeatable goods whose material history leads through a plastic-flower industry connected to Hong Kong, China’s manufacturing system, and global trade. They make circulation, replication, and the transfer of value physically present in the installation.

The data screen includes a study of the volume of low-value, duty-exempt parcels moving from China to the European Union across the period before COVID, through the pandemic, and into later customs-regime adjustments. The research does not treat parcel volume as neutral information. It renders platform pricing, tax thresholds, cross-border fulfilment, manufacturing competition, and interstate trade friction mutually legible. Cheapness is understood as a condition produced by law, logistics, labour, and algorithms; in industrial policy and geopolitics, that condition can become a means of coercion.

### ALL as urban infrastructure

The ALL footage records the final two days before the closure of a key Shanghai venue for underground and alternative electronic music. As the successor to The Shelter, ALL had sustained a specific ecology of music, bodies, nocturnal time, and informal exchange for eight years. Its closure is therefore not simply a loss of venue capacity. It makes visible the fragility of an urban infrastructure of temporary collectivity, at the point where it is absorbed by rent, regulation, platformised attention, and the city’s consumption of youth energy.

## Player, Void-Seeker, shelter

The project extends the User → Bot → Player → Void-Seeker trajectory into a concrete reality-political situation. A Player acts inside rules, can build, and can find a bug. A Void-Seeker looks for what a system does not name or cannot fully absorb.

Here, the Void is a **shelter**: a temporary refuge that appears in a moment of danger. The bullet-hell mechanic of grazing a bullet without dying gives this form. It is neither permanent escape nor a promise to overturn the system. It is a practiced moment of courage, self-recognition, and self-authorization: an opportunity to return to reality without surrendering all agency.

### Concept notes

- **Player / 玩家** — neither a game consumer nor a subject already free. A Player remains within rules and learns their rhythm, limits, and movable positions through action, trial, and judgment.
- **Void-Seeker / 虚空探索者** — a method for locating behaviours, affects, and relations not fully named, quantified, or absorbed by a system. The task is not to possess the Void, but to keep it perceptible.
- **Void / 虚空** — neither blankness, failure, nor utopia. In this project it is a momentary shelter. **Grazing** a bullet—Chinese: **擦弹**; Japanese: **グレイズ**—gives this shelter a concrete game form: proximity to danger without being hit.

The work keeps this possibility partly hidden. A viewer may reach a thought close to the artist's, or arrive elsewhere. The purpose is not a fixed answer but the thinking process activated by the encounter.
